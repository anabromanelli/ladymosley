Timeline

A chronological account of Lady Diana Mosley's life, family, residences, publications, friendships and major historical events.

1910–1928

Childhood & Early Years

17 June 1910

Born Diana Freeman-Mitford

Born Diana Freeman-Mitford, Belgravia, London.

1910–1919

Batsford Park

Family lives primarily at Batsford Park, Gloucestershire.

1916–1919

London During the War

Periodic stays in London; wartime household moves between Batsford and rented London houses.

1919

Asthall Manor

Family moves to Asthall Manor, Oxfordshire.

1919–1926

Childhood at Asthall

Diana’s formative childhood years at Asthall.

Early 1926

Paris

Family moves temporarily to Paris while renting their proprieties in England. There, she meets early family friends and intellectual visitors (Strachey, Huxley, Russell) through her mother’s social circle.

1926

Cours Féneelon

Diana attends Cours Féneelon, a Catholic day school (her only formal schooling).

Late 1926

Return to England

Family returns to England, Diana stays for the term.

Late 1926

Swinbrook House

Family moves into Swinbrook House, Oxfordshire, newly built by Lord Redesdale.

1926–1928

Late Adolescence

After having her dates in Paris discovered by her parents when she left her diary open, Diana is forbidden to return. Her late adolescence spent between Swinbrook and London.

Late 1927 / Early 1928

Evelyn Waugh

Diana meets Evelyn Waugh through the Guinness/Bright Young Things circle. Waugh becomes one of her closest friends; he later dedicates Vile Bodies (1930) to Diana and Bryan Guinness.

1928

Engagement to Bryan Walter Guinness

Secret engagement to Bryan Walter Guinness.

Late 1928

Bloomsbury-adjacent Literary World

Through Bryan, Diana meets members of the Bloomsbury-adjacent literary world, including Lytton Strachey and Dora Carrington, who admire her beauty and wit.

1929–1939

Marriage, Society & Germany

30 January 1929

Marriage to Bryan Guinness

Marries Bryan Guinness in London.

1929–1932

Guinness Circle

Social years hosting and attending parties with the Guinness Circle.

16 November 1930

Birth of Jonathan Bryan Guinness

Birth of Jonathan Bryan Guinness (later 3rd Baron Moyne).

1930

Biddesden

Sister Pamela moves into a cottage at Biddesden to run the farm.

8 September 1931

Birth of Desmond Walter Guinness

Birth of Desmond Walter Guinness (co-founder of the Irish Georgian Society).

1931–1932

Political and Literary Circles

Increasing contact with political and literary figures through Bryan’s circles.

1932

Meets Sir Oswald Mosley

Meets Sir Oswald Mosley; begins affair.

1932

Leaves Bryan Guinness

Leaves Bryan Guinness.

1932–1934

Divorce Proceedings

Divorce proceedings; legally finalised 1934.

1933

Death of Cynthia Mosley

Death of Cynthia Mosley.

1933–1936

Germany

Diana travels frequently to Germany; attends Nuremberg rallies.

6 October 1936

Marriage to Oswald Mosley

Marries Oswald Mosley in Berlin, the only way they could keep it from public knowledge as they did not want it to affect Mosley's political campaign.

27 November 1938

Birth of Alexander Mosley

Birth of Alexander Mosley.

13 April 1940

Birth of Max Mosley

Birth of Max Mosley.

1940–1959

War, Internment & France

23 May 1940

Arrest and Internment

Arrested under Defence Regulation 18B; imprisoned in Holloway Prison.

1941

Reunited with Mosley

Reunited with Mosley in a house within the prison grounds.

November 1943

Release

Released on health grounds.

1944–1946

Lives Quietly in England

Lives quietly in England under surveillance.

30 March 1945

Death of Tom Mitford

Death of Tom Mitford (Burma). Tom’s death was “a terrible blow,” and afterwards she felt the war had “taken the best of us,” leaving her with a quiet, permanent sense of loss.

28 May 1948

Death of Unity Mitford

Death of Unity Mitford. Unity’s long decline made her death “sad but not unexpected,” and that she remembered her chiefly as “the laughing girl at Swinbrook before everything went wrong.”

Late 1940s

Move to Paris

The Mosleys move to Paris, first in the Rue de Miromesnil area.

1951

Clonfert Palace

Diana and Oswald Mosley divide their time between Paris and Ireland, settling at Clonfert Palace, County Galway.

This move follows their post-war self-exile from England and precedes their permanent relocation to France.

1950s

Tatler Diaries

Writes Tatler diaries.

1953

Clonfert Palace Fire

Clonfert Palace catches fire and is severely damaged.

The fire effectively ends their Irish residence.

After the destruction of the house, the Mosleys leave Ireland and move permanently to France, first to Paris and later to Orsay.

1953–1959

The European

Edits The European.

1950s–1960s

Ireland

Frequent visits to Ireland to see Desmond.

17 February 1958

Death of Lord Redesdale

Death of Lord Redesdale. She described her father’s death as “the end of our childhood world.”

1960–1979

Writing, Family & Later Life

1960–1962

Paris and Orsay

Diana is living between Paris and Orsay, the Mosley family home outside the city.

1964–1966

Book Reviewing

Diana begins to take on more book reviewing, first informally, then more regularly. She becomes part of a small Parisian Anglo-French literary circle.

1 September 1963

Death of Sydney, Lady Redesdale

Sydney, Lady Redesdale, Diana’s mother, dies. Her death “left a great gap,” because Sydney had been “the centre of our early life,” and she felt the loss more deeply than she admitted aloud.

Diana travels to England for the funeral and spends time with her sisters at Swinbrook.

1967

Books & Bookmen

Diana begins reviewing for Books & Bookmen, a relationship that continues into the 1970s and 1980s. She also contributes occasional pieces to other literary periodicals.

1968

Ireland and the Irish Georgian Society

Diana spends time in Ireland visiting her son Desmond Guinness, who is now deeply involved in the Irish Georgian Society. She attends several architectural conservation events and house visits, though always privately and without publicity.

1969

Daily Life

Diana's routine consists of reviewing books, correspondence with literary friends, occasional travel to England and Ireland, and hosting small lunches and dinners. She also begins drafting material that will later appear in A Life of Contrasts (published 1977).

1971

Visits to England

Regular visits to England to see her sisters and to Swinbrook.

January 1973

Caring for Nancy Mitford and Oswald Mosley

Diana begins caring simultaneously for Nancy Mitford (dying in Versailles) and Oswald Mosley (declining at Orsay).

February–May 1973

Versailles and Orsay

Diana alternates between Versailles and Orsay, managing both households and medical care.

30 June 1973

Death of Nancy Mitford

Death of Nancy Mitford; Diana attends funeral and returns to Orsay to continue caring for Mosley. In her memoir she said Nancy’s death “left me desolate,” and that caring for her in Versailles had been “a privilege,” even though she never used dramatic language about grief.

Late 1973

Autobiography

Diana resumes work on her autobiography with renewed intensity.

1975

A Life of Contrasts

Diana completes the first full draft of A Life of Contrasts.

1976

Final Edits

Final edits of A Life of Contrasts delivered to her publisher.

1977

Publication of A Life of Contrasts

Publication of A Life of Contrasts.

1978

The Duchess of Windsor

Begins work on her next biographical project, later published in 1980 (The Duchess of Windsor).

1980–2003

Later Years, Writing & Legacy

3 December 1980

Death of Oswald Mosley

Death of Oswald Mosley at Orsay. Diana manages all funeral arrangements and correspondence. This marked a major change in her life and a long period of grief. Mosley’s death “ended a long companionship,” and she writes that after that, she simple "went on with things."

Late 1980

Move to Paris

Diana moves permanently to Paris, leaving Orsay behind.

This becomes her final home for the rest of her life.

1980

The Duchess of Windsor

Publication of The Duchess of Windsor, her biography of Wallis Simpson.

She spends the year responding to reviews and letters.

1981

The Pursuit of Laughter

Begins writing The Pursuit of Laughter, a collection of essays, memoir fragments, and biographical sketches.

1982

Visits to England

Regular visits to England to see her sisters and grandchildren.

1984

Publication of The Pursuit of Laughter

Publication of The Pursuit of Laughter.

The book receives attention for its sharp, unsentimental portraits and its elegant prose.

1985

Reviewing and England Visits

Diana’s reviewing work expands; she becomes a regular contributor to the Evening Standard.

Visits England several times; staying with Debo at Chatsworth.

24 April 1986

Death of Wallis, Duchess of Windsor

Death of Wallis, Duchess of Windsor, subject of Diana’s earlier biography.

Diana writes several private letters reflecting on the Duchess’s life and public image.

1989

Desert Island Discs

Appears on BBC Radio 4’s Desert Island Discs.

The interview causes controversy due to honesty and refusal to deny her opinion on Hitler at the time she knew her (pre-war).

6 July 1992

Death of Bryan Guinness

Death of Bryan Guinness, her first husband.

Diana writes private letters of condolence to members of the Guinness family.

12 April 1994

Death of Pamela Mitford

Death of Pamela Mitford, her sister.

Diana travels to England for the funeral and spends time with Deborah and Jessica.

22 July 1996

Death of Jessica Mitford

Death of Jessica Mitford, her sister.

Diana writes several letters reflecting on Jessica’s life and their political differences.

2000

Later Years

Health begins to decline modestly, but she remains socially active within her small Paris circle.

Early 2003

Final Months

Diana’s health deteriorates noticeably; she becomes largely house-bound, but still welcoming friends.

11 August 2003

Death of Diana Mosley

Diana Mosley dies in Paris, aged 93.

Burial at St Mary’s Church, Swinbrook, returning to the Oxfordshire landscape of her childhood.
